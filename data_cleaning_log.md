# Data cleaning log

Every cleaning decision for the Baltimore 311 data (2023–2025), with the number of rows each one affected.

The table below is generated by [`sql/03_cleaning.sql`](sql/03_cleaning.sql), which writes every count into the `clean.cleaning_log` table as it runs. No number in it is typed in by hand. The counts come from three places:

- **Steps 1–7** are counted on the raw data just before the clean table is built. The build makes those removals and fixes in a single pass.
- **Steps 8–13, 16, 25 and 27–36** are counted on the finished table.
- **Steps 14, 15, 17–24 and 26** are `UPDATE` fixes. Each logs its count in the same statement that makes the change, so the number is exactly what changed.

To see the log after running the pipeline:

```sql
SELECT * FROM clean.cleaning_log ORDER BY step;
```

## Before cleaning: a file-format fix

The city's CSV export leaves off the last two columns (`x`, `y`) on rows that have no map location: 295,297 rows in 2023, 319,383 in 2024 and 259,577 in 2025 (874,257 in total). PostgreSQL's loader rejects rows with too few fields, so [`scripts/fix_ragged_rows.py`](scripts/fix_ragged_rows.py) pads those rows with empty fields as the file streams into the database. No values are changed, and every file's row count after loading matches the city's published count exactly.

## Why these decisions

**Flag, don't delete.** Only 2 rows were removed (the test records). Everything else that would distort a measure is kept and flagged, so each analysis can decide what to include. For example, call-center questions count toward "how many people contacted 311", but not toward "how fast does the city close requests".

**"Resolution time" means time to close.** It is the close date minus the created date. Closed does not always mean fixed. Among the requests used for resolution times, 46.3% have the outcome "Work completed" and 33.5% were "SR closed by agency-specific work management system" (no detail). 3.6% were "assessed and no cause for action determined", 1.8% "Work could not be completed", and 6.6% have no outcome. Requests closed because the work was referred to another agency are left out, like transfers, because closing them measures the hand-off.

**Booked pickups and events (step 29).** Residents book bulk-trash and appliance pickups, leaf collection and Community Pitch-In events for a future date. The city sets the due date to 11:59 PM on the booked day, and most close on that day: 94.9% of weekday and 94.5% of Saturday bulk pickups. Their medians, 42.9 and 39.9 days, are the wait for the booking, not how fast the city responds, so they are left out of resolution times.

**Crew-logged work (step 28).** City crews log some work themselves after doing it. Examples are the four "Proactive" types (rat baiting, street and alley cleaning, graffiti removal) and "TRM-Pickup Pothole", which is 100% entered internally, with 93.8% of requests closed within a minute. These are not resident reports, and their "time to close" is meaningless.

**Request types (step 13).** The city's type names start with a division code (`TRM-Potholes`). The new `request_type` column drops the code, so the city's 328 published types (326 after removing the 2 test types) become 308 names. The original name stays in `sr_type`. 17 names appear under more than one code:

- 5 are the same work moving between Transportation divisions: Flex posts, Sign Damaged/Sign Structure, Steel Plate Complaint, Street and Crosswalk Markings, Street Cut Issues.
- 4 pair a call-center or police version with a field version: Community Pitch-In, Escalation, Grass Mowing, Miscellaneous Request.
- 3 span two agencies: Conduit Observation, Graffiti Removal, Ombudsman Review.
- 5 span Finance sub-units: Other, Paid Wrong Account/Misapplied Payment, Payment Not Posted, Refunds, Transfer Request from Another Account.

Rankings by type leave out call-center requests, so the call-center versions don't mix in. Use `sr_type` when the division matters.

**Status.** The published status mixes two things: where the request stands (Open, Closed and so on) and why it was closed (Duplicate, Transferred), with inconsistent spelling. The cleaned data separates them into `status` plus yes/no flags. Status is treated as the final word on whether a request is open, so a close date on an open request (step 23) is removed.

**Agency (steps 14–16).** `TRS-Parking Complaint` has an agency on every 2023 row, but the field came through blank on 21,496 of 37,501 rows in 2024 and 50,584 of 51,287 in 2025. A missing agency is filled only when one agency accounts for at least 90% of the same request type's recorded rows, and the original blank is kept in `agency_raw`. Separately, 2,516 field requests carried the call center's agency code "ECC" (mostly parking and Mayor's Office types). They get the agency most often recorded for their division code.

**Old neighborhood names (step 18).** In 2023 the city used a mix of older and current neighborhood names. Each old name was matched by taking the street addresses filed under it in 2023 and finding the neighborhood each address was most often filed under in 2024–2025. After all fixes, every neighborhood in the data is one of the 279 on the city's official list, so it joins cleanly to the 2020 population table.

**Missing neighborhoods are mostly expected (steps 31–34).** 919,904 rows have no neighborhood:

- **874,257 have no map point,** mostly people calling 311 with a question.
- **38,472 have a map point outside Baltimore City.** The city's water utility also serves Baltimore County, so county water problems appear in city 311 data. The "outside" test uses the outermost latitude and longitude of every request that does have a city neighborhood (latitude 39.1992 to 39.3720, longitude -76.7113 to -76.5297). The raw addresses confirm it: 33,272 of these rows name a county place such as Parkville, Dundalk, Pikesville or Catonsville, and only 139 say "Baltimore City".
- **6,679 are inside that box but have no neighborhood or council district.** The box is a rectangle, so its corners take in some county land. 3,116 of these addresses name a place other than Baltimore, against 3,186 that say "Baltimore City".
- **496 have a council district but no neighborhood,** so they are in the city.

**Dates (steps 22–26).**

- **Due dates.** On types with no service target (972,394 of the 989,917 affected rows are call-center), the export shifts the due date back by exactly the UTC offset, 5 hours in winter and 4 in summer (989,916 of the 989,917). For example, request 23-00000001 was created `1/1/2023 12:05:49 AM` with due date `12/31/2022 7:05:49 PM`. These due dates are removed.
- **Close times without seconds.** About 31% of close times are stored without seconds (e.g. `3:58:00 PM`), against 1.6% of created times. A request closed in the same minute it was created can therefore look a few seconds early. All 26,845 such rows have a close time on a whole minute, and they count as closed in 0 time.
- **Closed before created.** The remaining 4,555 rows really do close earlier than the minute they were created. They are kept, with no resolution time.

## Notes and limitations

- **April–May 2025 are mostly missing from the city's file.** From 2025-03-31 to 2025-06-01 the city's request counter moved 193,674 numbers, but only 48,423 of those requests (25.0%) are in the 2025 file. In the two months before and after, 99.0% and 99.3% are present, so about 145,000 requests are missing. April 2025 has 14,866 requests against 93,855 in April 2024, and May 2025 has 31,665 against 98,471. The published rows from the gap are also unusual: 10.4% of field requests are still open, against 2.0% in the rest of 2025. These rows are flagged (`in_2025_data_gap`), left out of resolution times, and marked in monthly charts. The cause, probably an export problem, is unconfirmed. The raw file has the same counts, so the cleaning did not cause it.
- **Council districts were redrawn between the 2024 and 2025 files.** Comparing the same map points, 2023 and 2024 assign a different council district only 0.03% of the time, but 2024 and 2025 do so 7.05% of the time. The population table matches the new map, so per-capita rates by council district are only valid for 2025 requests. Neighborhood boundaries did not change this way, so multi-year comparisons use neighborhoods.
- **8 neighborhoods had 0 residents in the 2020 Census** (parks, cemeteries, industrial areas) but hold 5,804 requests. Per-capita rates leave out very small neighborhoods so these don't top the ranking.
- **Times are Baltimore local time** with no time zone attached. In 2025, requests peak at 1 PM and bottom out at 3 AM, which fits local time. Resolution times that span a daylight-saving change can be off by one hour.
- **"Still open" depends on when the city last updated each file.** The city last updated the 2023 file in January 2025, the 2024 file in December 2025, and the 2025 file on the day of download (2026-10-15). A 2023 request marked Open means open as of January 2025.
- **Resolution time** is calculated by PostgreSQL as a generated column, so it always matches the dates.
